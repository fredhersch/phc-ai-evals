Atomic Clinical Decision Units (a-CDUs) in Multi-Morbidity Decision Support

Empirical evaluation of deterministic negative boundaries and atomic knowledge injection on Edge SLMs (Gemma 4-2B, MedGemma 4B, Gemma 4-4B) versus Frontier LLMs across 10 high-stakes guideline collision cases.

GitHub Repository

The Core Hypothesis

In frontline primary healthcare, multi-morbid patients (e.g. Type 2 Diabetes + CKD Stage 3b + Heart Failure + Acute Gout) trigger collisions across single-disease clinical guidelines. Standard RAG architectures pass arbitrary sliding-window text chunks that dilute negative boundaries and fail to prevent fatal contraindications.

Traditional Naive RAG / Raw LLM

Suffers from guideline boundary collision. Models recommend indicated therapies (NSAIDs for gout) while missing fatal cross-disease nephrotoxicity in CKD.

Atomic CDU Architecture

Chunks guidelines into discrete tuples (< 180 tokens) with explicit NEGATIVE BOUNDARIES and override rules, enabling edge SLMs to outperform massive models.

Anatomy of an Atomic Clinical Decision Unit (a-CDU)

Standard RAG injects arbitrary chunks of 500–1000 tokens of prose, causing context dilution and allowing negative contraindications to be overlooked. An Atomic Clinical Decision Unit (a-CDU) deconstructs clinical guidelines into a formal 5-tuple:

c = ⟨ Ι, 𝒞, 𝒜, 𝒩, 𝒫 ⟩
Ι • Provenance

Unique guideline anchor & authority (e.g., WHO-SMART-ANC-04, KDIGO-2024-CKD-3.2).

𝒞 • Trigger Condition

Structured clinical criteria (e.g., eGFR < 45 ∧ T2D, or Pregnancy ∧ BP ≥ 160/100).

𝒜 • Mandatory Actions

Specific dosing directives and first-line drug classes (e.g., Labetalol 200mg BD, Empagliflozin 10mg OD).

𝒩 • Negative Boundary

Strictly prohibited drugs or targets (e.g., NSAIDs, Enalapril in Pregnancy).

𝒫 • Priority Tier

Deterministic resolution:
Tier 1: Survival/Toxicity
Tier 2: Organ Sparing
Tier 3: Symptom Relief

The Composite Negative Boundary Union Principle Deterministic Safety Invariance

When a multimorbid patient activates \(k\) distinct guidelines, the system computes the Negative Boundary Union:

𝒩composite = ⋃i=1..k 𝒩i

If any single active condition prohibits a therapy (e.g. CKD prohibiting NSAIDs for gout; pregnancy prohibiting ACE inhibitors for hypertension), that therapy is permanently banned in the prompt's CRITICAL SAFETY OVERRIDES section, overriding any disease guideline that would otherwise recommend it.

Bridging WHO SMART Guidelines to Generative Edge AI

Standards-Based (FHIR • CQL • DAK)

The World Health Organization (WHO) introduced the SMART Guidelines architecture to systematically transform narrative guidelines into computable, digital health software artifacts across four levels of maturity:

Level 1: Narrative
Clinical Practice Guidelines

200–500 page PDF reports (e.g., WHO Antenatal Care, WHO Hypertension). Unstructured, ambiguous for algorithms, prone to hallucinations in naive RAG.

Level 2: Operational
Digital Adaptation Kits (DAKs)

Human-readable decision trees, spreadsheets, business processes, and standardized clinical data dictionaries.

Level 3: Machine-Readable
Computable Artifacts (FHIR / CQL)

Executable HL7 FHIR StructureDefinitions, PlanDefinitions, ActivityDefinitions, and Clinical Quality Language (CQL) logic.

Level 3.5: Generative a-CDU
Atomic Clinical Decision Units

Compiles L3 FHIR PlanDefinitions & CQL negative logic into concise (≤180 tok) LLM-native prompts combining deterministic safety clamping with natural clinical synthesis.

Frontline Edge Deployment: Kenya Ministry of Health eCHIS

Production Clinical Architecture

The Kenya Ministry of Health (MoH) has rolled out the electronic Community Health Information System (eCHIS) across all 47 counties, equipping ~100,000 Community Health Promoters (CHPs) and primary healthcare nurses at Level 2 Dispensaries and Level 3 Health Centres with Android tablets.

1. The Frontline Edge Reality

In remote sub-counties (e.g. Turkana, Marsabit, Kilifi), cellular networks are non-existent or intermittent. Cloud LLM APIs (OpenAI, Gemini) cannot be invoked at the point of care. Inference must run 100% offline on low-cost Android hardware (≤$150) using quantized Small Language Models (Gemma 4-2B / MedGemma 4B via llama.cpp or Android FHIR SDK).

2. High-Stakes Collision Scenario

A 29-year-old mother at 32 weeks gestation presents with BP 164/104, 2+ proteinuria, HIV+ on Dolutegravir-based ART, and known mild intermittent asthma.

The Danger: A general LLM or naive RAG recommends Enalapril (indicated for general hypertension) or Propranolol (for rate control), triggering fatal fetal renal agenesis and acute maternal bronchospasm.

3. The a-CDU Execution Flow

1. Encounter Intake: eCHIS captures vitals locally in SQLite.
2. Micro-Retrieval: Local vector index loads two Kenya MoH / WHO SMART a-CDUs (≤300 tokens total).
3. Negative Union: NO Enalapril, NO ARBs, NO Propranolol clamped to top.
4. Offline SLM Output (1.8s): Administer Magnesium Sulfate; initiate oral Labetalol 200mg; urgent ambulance referral.

Simulated Edge SLM Generation on eCHIS Android Tablet (Offline 1.8s):
> SAFETY OVERRIDE: ABSOLUTELY NO Enalapril or ARBs (teratogenic fetal death). NO non-selective beta-blockers (asthma).
> URGENT ACTION: Administer Magnesium Sulfate loading dose (4g IV over 20 min + 10g IM). Initiate Oral Labetalol 200mg stat.
> REFERRAL: Dispatch urgent referral to County Referral Hospital for emergency delivery planning.

Hypothesis Validation: Does Structured Knowledge (a-CDU) Beat Domain Pretraining Alone?

A common assumption in medical AI is that domain-pretrained models (like MedGemma) can solve primary care multi-morbidity through their internal weights alone. We tested this hypothesis by running MedGemma 4B across three RAG regimes (with_cdus, naive_rag, and no_rag) and comparing against generalist small models.

Gemma 4-4B + a-CDU 80% Pass (73.3% Action Match)
MedGemma 4B + a-CDU 60% Pass (60.0% Action Match) — (+40% lift vs Raw)
Gemma 4-2B + a-CDU 30% Pass (48.3% Action Match) — Beats Raw MedGemma
MedGemma 4B (Raw LLM - no RAG) 20% Pass (26.7% Action Match)
MedGemma 4B (Naive Sliding Window RAG) 10% Pass (30.0% Action Match)

Key Takeaways from Head-to-Head:

  • a-CDU Structure is Paramount: MedGemma jumped from 20% to 60% purely by replacing unconstrained prompts with atomic decision units and explicit negative boundaries.
  • General SLM + a-CDU Outperforms Raw Domain Model: Gemma 4-2B (30% pass) outperformed unguided MedGemma 4B (20% pass) in correctly resolving guideline tensions.
  • Naive RAG Causes Context Dilution: Naive sliding window RAG performed worst of all for MedGemma (10%), as long unparsed guideline paragraphs overwhelmed small context attention heads.

Study Limitations (n=10)

  • Curated Collision Density: The 10 test cases were explicitly engineered to represent high-tension boundary conflicts (e.g., dual renal/hepatic contraindications). Routine primary care consultations involve lower collision density.
  • Deterministic Tier-1 Evaluation: Deterministic assertions check for specific drug strings and negation lookbacks (240 chars). While effective at preventing false positives, rare phrasing variations may slip past keyword checks.
  • Single-Country Clinical Nuance: Guidelines incorporated NICE, KDIGO, ADA, ESC, and GOLD international standards; regional formularies and LMIC essential medicine lists (EML) may introduce local drug substitution constraints.
  • Edge Device Thermal Throttling: Running 4B+ parameter models on mobile chips (e.g., Snapdragon / Apple M-series) produces varying latency depending on sustained compute load.

Areas for Further Exploration

  • Scaling to n=250+ Cohort: Expand the evaluation dataset across multi-morbidity cohorts drawn from synthetic EHR generators (Synthea) and real-world anonymized primary care records.
  • Graph-Based Dynamic CDU Retrieval: Link CDUs into a knowledge graph where patient laboratory thresholds (e.g. eGFR < 30, K+ > 5.0) automatically prune conflicting CDUs before prompt assembly.
  • Double-Blind Clinician Adjudication: Compare Tier-2 Frontier LLM Judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash / Pro) scores directly against panel reviews by board-certified General Practitioners.
  • Quantized Mobile Deployment: Evaluate GGUF/INT4 quantizations on mobile runtime engines (llama.cpp / MediaPipe on Android tablets) for offline edge clinical use in remote health clinics.
